Introduction and Project Objectives
This Mini Project is a crucial component of the DSAI5102_20261 Principles of Data Science course. It is designed to push students to apply the theoretical knowledge acquired in class to real-world data scenarios. The project aims to deepen students' understanding of  data science techniques through practical application, encouraging independent thinking, teamwork, and the ability to extract valuable insights from data.

 

Project Format and Scope
Students may choose one of the following two formats for their Mini Project:

 

2.1 Format 1: Data Application and Practice

Objective: Select a practical data scenario of interest.
Data: Utilize open-source datasets, public datasets, or self-collected proprietary datasets.
Content: Apply statistical data mining, machine learning, deep learning, or other relevant methods covered in the course to conduct in-depth analysis, modeling, prediction, or classification.
Deliverable: Generate meaningful insights from the data analysis and model results, and propose solutions to a practical problem.
 

2.2 Format 2: Academic Paper Reproduction

Objective: Select an academic paper related to statistical data mining or machine learning.
Requirement: The paper must contain core statistical data mining or machine learning methodologies.
Content: Attempt to reproduce the key experimental results, core algorithms, or methods presented in the paper.
Deliverable: Validate the effectiveness of the paper's methodology and discuss the challenges encountered and potential areas for improvement during the reproduction process.

Grading Principle

The final score will be based on the overall group output, combined with an assessment of each member's individual contribution. Fair and effective teamwork is highly encouraged.

Evaluation Criteria and Grading Rubric
The Mini Project will be evaluated based on the following six key aspects to ensure project quality and learning effectiveness:

 

Evaluation Dimension

Key Focus

Detailed Description

Time

Oral Presentation Duration

The presentation must strictly adhere to the allocated time limit. Penalties will be applied for exceeding or significantly underutilizing the time.

Team

Division of Labor and Contribution

The project report must clearly outline the team members' roles, individual contributions, and collaboration methods. Evaluates the effectiveness of teamwork.

Slides

Visual and Content Presentation

Slides should be clear, professional, aesthetically pleasing, and logically structured to effectively support the oral presentation.

Completeness

Achievement of Objectives

The extent to which the project meets its pre-set goals, and whether all stages—data processing, model training, and results analysis—are complete.

Methodology

Method Selection and Application

Appropriateness of the chosen methods, soundness of the theoretical foundation, rigor of the application process, and reasonableness of the result interpretation.

Novelty

Topic and Method Innovation

Originality of the topic, uniqueness of the data source, improvement of existing methods, or application to a novel scenario.

Suggestions and Notes
Topic Selection: It is recommended to choose a topic that is closely related to the course content and of moderate difficulty, ensuring high-quality completion within the project timeline.
Data: Ensure the legality and reliability of the data. Clearly state the data source and pre-processing steps in the report.
Report: The written report should have a complete structure, including an introduction, data description, methodology, experimental results, discussion, and conclusion.
Contribution: Team members should include a Statement of Individual Contribution at the end of the written report, which will be used by the TA for assessing individual scores.
